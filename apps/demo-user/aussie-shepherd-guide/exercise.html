How Much Exercise Does an Aussie Need?

Plan on 1–2 hours of vigorous exercise every single day. That's not a leisurely stroll around the block — think jogging, hiking, fetch, swimming, or off-leash time in a securely fenced area. These dogs were bred to work all day alongside a rancher, and their bodies (and minds) expect that level of activity. A once-a-day walk simply won't cut it for most Aussies, especially working-line dogs.

If an Aussie doesn't get enough outlet for that energy, you'll likely see it come out sideways — excessive barking, destructive chewing, obsessive herding of the household, or anxiety. The good news: give them a job, and they're some of the most content, well-behaved dogs around.

Agility, Herding Trials, and Dog Sports

Aussies excel at structured dog sports. Agility courses tap into their speed, precision, and handler focus. Herding trials let them do the job they were bred for, even without a working flock at home. Flyball, dock diving, disc dog competitions, and nose work are all great outlets too — and many Aussie owners find that training for a sport gives their dog exactly the combination of physical and mental exercise it craves.

Positive-Reinforcement Training Tips

  • Start early. Begin basic obedience and socialization as soon as you bring your puppy home — Aussies are quick learners and eager to engage.
  • Reward-based methods work best. Treats, praise, and play motivate Aussies far more effectively than corrections. Harsh training can create a fearful or shut-down dog.
  • Keep sessions short and varied. Five to ten focused minutes, several times a day, beats one long drilling session — this breed gets bored with repetition.
  • Redirect herding behavior. Teach a solid "leave it" and provide legitimate herding outlets rather than trying to eliminate the instinct entirely.
  • Socialize deliberately. Because Aussies are naturally reserved with strangers, planned exposure to new people, dogs, and environments during puppyhood pays off for life.
