Frequently Asked Questions

Are Australian Shepherds good with kids?

Generally, yes — Aussies are affectionate, loyal, and protective of their families, including children. That said, their herding instinct can lead them to nip at heels or try to "herd" running kids, so early training and supervision around young children is important.

Do Australian Shepherds bark a lot?

Aussies can be vocal, especially when under-exercised, bored, or trying to alert you to something. Dogs that get enough daily physical and mental exercise, and are trained with a solid "quiet" cue, tend to bark far less than those left to their own devices.

Can Australian Shepherds live in an apartment?

It's possible but challenging. Aussies need 1–2 hours of vigorous daily exercise and enough mental stimulation to keep that busy brain satisfied — apartment living can work if you're committed to getting them out for serious exercise every day, but it's not the ideal setup for this breed compared to a home with yard access.

Are Australian Shepherds hypoallergenic?

No. Aussies have a double coat that sheds year-round, with heavier seasonal shedding twice a year. They are not considered hypoallergenic, and people with significant dog allergies should spend time with the breed before committing.

How much grooming do Australian Shepherds need?

Weekly brushing is the baseline, with more frequent brushing (sometimes daily) during seasonal "blow coat" periods when the undercoat sheds heavily. Regular grooming keeps mats at bay and helps you spot skin issues early.

How much exercise do Australian Shepherds need?

Plan on 1–2 hours of vigorous activity daily — running, hiking, fetch, agility, or herding work. This is a high-energy working breed, and under-exercised Aussies often develop behavior problems.

Are Australian Shepherds easy to train?

Yes, in the sense that they're extremely intelligent and quick to learn — but they also need consistent, engaging, positive-reinforcement training. A bored or under-stimulated Aussie can become willful or find its own (usually less desirable) ways to entertain itself.

What health problems are common in Australian Shepherds?

The most notable concerns are hip dysplasia, MDR1 drug sensitivity, and progressive retinal atrophy (PRA). Genetic testing and health screening from a reputable breeder can help you understand and manage these risks.

How big do Australian Shepherds get?

Adults typically stand 18–23 inches at the shoulder and weigh 40–65 pounds, with males on average larger than females.

Where should I adopt or buy an Australian Shepherd?

Check local Australian Shepherd rescues first — there are wonderful adult dogs and even puppies in need of homes. If you do go through a breeder, choose one who health tests for hips, eyes, and MDR1, and who lets you meet the puppy's parents.
