Grooming the Double Coat

Photo: Andrea Arbogast / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0)

Aussies have a weather-resistant double coat — a coarser outer layer over a soft, insulating undercoat. Plan on brushing at least once a week to prevent matting and distribute skin oils, paying extra attention to the feathering behind the ears, on the legs, and around the britches.

Twice a year, during seasonal shedding (often called "blowing coat"), that undercoat releases in earnest. During these weeks, daily brushing with an undercoat rake makes a real difference — both for your dog's comfort and for your furniture.

Key Health Considerations

Hip Dysplasia

Like many medium-to-large active breeds, Aussies can be prone to hip dysplasia, a malformation of the hip joint that can lead to arthritis and pain over time. Reputable breeders screen breeding stock with OFA or PennHIP hip evaluations.

MDR1 Drug Sensitivity

A significant portion of Australian Shepherds carry a mutation in the MDR1 gene, which affects how the body clears certain drugs (including some common ivermectin-based parasite preventatives and anesthetics) from the bloodstream. Dogs with this mutation can have severe, even life-threatening reactions to standard doses. A simple genetic test can identify carriers so your vet can adjust medications accordingly.

Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA)

PRA is an inherited condition that causes the retina to degenerate gradually, eventually leading to blindness. It's recessive and can be screened for with genetic testing before breeding.

Genetic Testing Advice

If you're getting a puppy from a breeder, ask to see health clearances for hips, eyes (CERF/OFA eye exam), and DNA panel results including MDR1 status. Responsible breeders test proactively and can explain exactly what each result means for your future dog.
